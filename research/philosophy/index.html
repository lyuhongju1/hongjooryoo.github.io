Philosophy of Explanation and Understanding

I have proposed Foundational Explanationism (FE), forthcoming in Ergo. Explanationism holds that successful scientific explanations license ontological commitment to the entities and structures they invoke. FE restricts that inference: it is warranted only when it targets pre-emergent, metaphysically fundamental structures and entities. At emergent levels, explanatory success is better accounted for by structural fit, empirical adequacy, or integration with broader theory. I show how FE works in cases from quantum gravity, effective field theory, and gauge symmetry.

Earlier, I developed the context-dependent mapping (fc mapping) framework as an extension to Strevens' kairetic account of scientific explanation and Railton's DNP account for simple probabilities, in an attempt to elucidate how varying experimental or contextual conditions lead to distinct manifestations of phenomena, particularly in quantum mechanics and general relativity. This approach allows for a nuanced understanding and accommodation of complementarities in these fields, emphasizing the influence of specific contexts on observational outcomes. Currently, I am investigating the nature of incomplete laws in science and the notion of constraints within explanation.
